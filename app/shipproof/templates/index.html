Questions people actually ask

Do I need to connect GitHub?

No. The URL scan works on its own. Connecting a public repo adds migration, route-handler and dependency checks — but it is optional, and we never ask for write access.

Is this a penetration test?

No, and we will not pretend it is. We do a passive check of what your app serves to the public internet, plus a guided prompt-injection test you run yourself. A full pentest is a different, much more expensive engagement.

Will you scan apps that are not mine?

No. You have to confirm you own the app or have permission. We rate-limit scans and we do not run exploitation attempts.

Does a good score mean my app is unhackable?

No — nobody honest can promise that. A check is a point in time. That is why the badge has an expiry date and why the Watch plan re-scans after every deploy.

What exactly do you store?

The report: findings, evidence snippets and the target URL. We do not keep your source code, and we do not run or store your database. Reports are deleted after 30 days unless you keep the Watch plan.

My app is still in beta. Is it too early?

That is the best time. The scan is free, and the cheapest bug to fix is the one nobody has exploited yet.

I am not technical. Will I understand the report?

That is the design goal. Every finding says what it is, why it matters, and what to do — plus a copy-paste prompt you can hand to the AI that built the app.

What if I disagree with a finding?

Mark it and tell us. False positives in a security report are worse than no report, and our detection is deliberately conservative.
